Minor risk

  • Near Miss
    A situation where an injury could have occurred given a slight shift in time or position
  • First Aid
    An injury that did not require treatment by a medical practitioner.

Major risk

  • Restricted Activity
    An injury or illness where a healthcare professional recommended that the employee should not perform their normal routine job functions or work their full scheduled working day.
  • Lost time
    An injury or illness where the employee is not able to return to work on their next scheduled (full) shift as a result of their sustained injuries.

Hazardous

  • Life Threatening
    An injury that requires immediate life-preserving rescue action. These cases usually require the intervention of emergency response personnel to provide life-saving support. Some common examples include significant blood loss, damage to the brain or spinal cord, use of CPR, chest or abdominal trauma affecting vital organs and serious burns.

Catastrophic

  • Life Altering
    An injury that result in a permanent and significant loss of a major body part or organ function that permanently changes or disables the employee's normal life activity. Some examples include significant head injuries, spinal cord injuries, paralysis, major amputations, catastrophic fractured bones, and serious burns.
  • Fatality
    Accidents where the employee dies as a direct result of the injury or illness. {!
